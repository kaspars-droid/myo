Myo Calc — Support

For Myo Calc 1.4 on iPhone, iPad and Mac.

Myo Calc is a line based calculator: you write on the left, the answers appear on the right, and the whole thing is a plain text file you own.

Getting in touch

Email: kaspars@cirsis.lv

Bugs and requests: github.com/kaspars-droid/myo/issues

Myo Calc is made by one person, so an answer may take a few days rather than a few minutes. If something is wrong, the useful things to say are: which device and which version of iOS or macOS, what you typed, what appeared beside it, and what you expected instead. A sheet is plain text, so pasting the lines themselves into the message is usually the fastest way to show the problem.

The first line names the sheet, which is why it is also the file's name. The bar along the bottom totals the whole sheet, and each currency is totalled on its own, since the app holds no exchange rates and will not invent one.

Answers are written the way your device writes numbers. If your region uses a comma for decimals, then you can type 12,50 as well as 12.50, and the answers come back in the same style.

Where your sheets are kept

A sheet is a plain text file ending in .myocalc. It opens in any text editor and stays readable without this app, which is the point of it.

The app keeps its sheets in a folder you choose. Until you choose one they go in the app's own Documents folder, which on iPhone and iPad the Files app shows under On My iPhone → Myo Calc. They are ordinary files there too, and choosing a folder later moves them into it rather than leaving them behind.

On iPhone and iPad the app also holds its own copy of whatever folder you chose, so sheets open instantly and still work with no signal. That copy is kept where the Files app does not show it, so a sheet appears once — where it lives, not twice.

The same sheets on more than one device

Point each device at the same folder in a service that syncs — iCloud Drive, Dropbox, Google Drive, or anything else that appears as a folder. The app writes ordinary files, so the sync is the service's job rather than the app's.

One thing to know before you rely on it: there is no merging. If the same sheet is edited on two devices at once, the last write wins and the other version is gone. Editing one sheet in one place at a time is safe; editing it in two places at once is not.

Google Drive, Dropbox, and folders that cannot be chosen

On iPhone and iPad, Google Drive, Dropbox and Box appear greyed out in the folder picker and cannot be selected. That is their decision rather than this app's: a service has to declare that its folders may be picked, and those do not. iCloud Drive, On My iPhone, network shares and USB drives all can be. On a Mac the picker can choose them, because macOS presents them as ordinary folders on disk.

Sheets from those services can be added one at a time instead. In the menu, choose Add Sheets… and pick the ones you want. Each stays where it is: the app reads it, writes your edits back to it, and keeps it in the list from then on, marked with a small cloud.

What you do not get is discovery. The app was granted that one file and nothing around it, so a sheet added to that folder from another device will not turn up on its own — add it the same way when you want it.

The picker offers every file, not only sheets, because those services do not always say what a .myocalc file is; asked only for sheets, it would grey out the very files you were looking for. If you pick something that is not text, the app says Not a sheet and adds nothing.

Deleting a sheet

On a Mac, every sheet in the list has an X on the right. Press it once and it becomes a red trash can; press that and the sheet is deleted. It goes to the Trash, so a delete you did not mean is one keystroke to undo in the Finder.

On iPhone and iPad, open the menu, choose Delete Sheet and pick a sheet. The menu then asks Delete name? in red, and tapping that carries it out. Two presses on both platforms, on purpose, and both forget again after a few seconds if you go no further.

A sheet added one at a time from Google Drive or Dropbox is only removed from the list. The file stays in the service you put it in: the app was never granted permission to delete anything there, and does not.

Emptying a sheet and moving to another one deletes it as well — a sheet with nothing left in it has been finished with, and without this a folder fills up with blank ones. A cloud sheet is the exception, because emptying it is an edit like any other and is written back as one.

If something looks wrong

  • A line has no answer. The app leaves a line alone when it cannot read it as a sum, so prose stays prose. Check for a stray word inside the figures.
  • The total looks too small. total only adds what was written since the last blank line, and a named figure such as rate = 0.21 is deliberately kept out of the totals.
  • A sheet is missing. Look in the folder itself with Files or Finder. The sheets are ordinary files, so if they are there, they are not lost, whatever the app is showing.
  • A cloud sheet did not arrive. Open the folder in the service's own app first and let it download, then reopen Myo Calc.
  • The folder you want is greyed out. Some services do not allow their folders to be picked at all. Add those sheets one at a time instead — see above.
  • A sheet added one at a time is out of date. Only that one file was shared with the app, so it follows that file and nothing else. If the sheet was replaced rather than edited over there, add it again.

What you need to run it

  • iPhone and iPad: iOS or iPadOS 17 or later.
  • Mac: macOS 15 or later.

Privacy

Myo Calc collects nothing, has no accounts and makes no network connections. The full statement is at kaspars-droid.github.io/myo/privacy/.
